Copyrights for Humans

An Affirmation of Human Creativity, Authorship, and the Rights of Those Who Create

❦

We, the undersigned, affirm a simple but fundamental principle: Copyright begins with human creativity.

Under United States law, an eligible original work of human authorship generally receives copyright protection automatically when it is fixed in a tangible form. Copyright does not have to be purchased or created by government registration. Registration provides important additional legal benefits, but it is not the source of the underlying copyright.

As artificial intelligence becomes increasingly capable of producing music, images, literature, and other material, this distinction deserves to be understood and preserved.

We therefore proclaim:

  1. Human authorship matters. Copyright protects original human creative expression. Using technology does not erase the human contributions that deserve recognition.
  2. Copyright begins with creation. Eligible work is protected when created and fixed; registration provides additional benefits but does not create the underlying copyright.
  3. AI is not automatically an author. Machine-generated material alone does not establish human authorship. When people use AI creatively, their original human contributions may qualify for protection.
  4. Human rights must remain clear. As technology advances, we welcome careful discussion about whether laws and constitutions need greater clarity concerning human dignity, personhood and self-determination, without diminishing existing rights.
  5. Let the conversation begin. We invite creators, workers, educators, lawmakers and the public to help protect human creativity while exploring technology's possibilities.

Our declaration: We do not reject technology. We recognize its possibilities. We affirm that human creativity is foundational to copyright and that people should remain central to laws protecting creative work.
